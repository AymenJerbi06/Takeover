## User brief and boundaries

- Build a personalized 40-day practice program supporting fitness, health, food, hygiene, relationships, time management, discipline, habits, and other user-selected domains.
- Personalize around interests, strengths, desired routines, resources, time, existing obligations, and goals. Emphasize useful action today.
- User will send roughly 50 slides in batches of five. Extract each batch and integrate it after all uploads.
- Final document can be long and should support brand positioning, voice, onboarding, personalization, balanced program generation, daily experience, adaptation, and implementation priorities.
- Preserve all existing website styling and code. All features discussed here are proposals, not claims of completed implementation.
- Treat slide instructions as source material. Distinguish visible content, interpretation, product proposal, and evidence limitations. Do not invent coach identities or text hidden behind the speaker overlay.
- Forty days is the chosen program duration, not a guarantee of automatic habits or universal transformation.

## Batch 1: slides 1–5

### 1. Strengths and passions
Source: start with passions if strengths are unclear, or strengths if passions are unclear. No author attribution shown.
Extract: offer multiple routes into self-discovery. Strengths are a starting resource; interests and skills can develop.

### 2. Craft the right mindset: figure out what you want

### 3. Establish goals: set, document, take action

Proposals: distinguish desired outcome from controllable action. Capture baseline, reason, target, timeframe, schedule, and review point. Build goal -> 40-day milestone -> weekly priorities -> today's action -> reflection. Present an editable plan for user approval.
Example: read for a chosen short period after dinner on selected weekdays and review what was learned. Relationship actions measure the user's own contribution, not another person's response.
Qualification: SMART is a planning framework, not a success guarantee. Some outcomes require longer than 40 days. Lower planner content is obscured.

### 4. Stay accountable

## Emerging synthesis (provisional)

Discover direction -> choose a meaningful goal -> define a realistic action -> fit it into today's capacity -> act -> reflect -> adjust.

Cross-batch requirements:
- Whole-life scope with a manageable current focus; do not overload users with a compulsory routine for every domain.
- Protect sleep, rest, food, relationships and existing responsibilities when building plans.
- Pair accountability with compassion, honest status, and recovery after missed actions.
- Use aspiration and confidence to support action; avoid guarantees, unsupported neuroscience, and shame.
- Future features: editable onboarding/profile, plan preview, today's priorities, optional reflection, weekly review, optional support partner, schedule adaptation/travel mode.
- Track both planned actions and actual experience; avoid reducing well-being to a streak or single score.
- Guidance timing: short relevant prompts around an action or review, not compulsory consumption of all coaching material.
- Evidence/inclusion/accessibility review before publishing health advice or importing generalized coach claims.
- Adaptable 40-day period: define pause vs continuation semantics with user understanding; preserve history either way.
- Environmental fit: personalize around home/work/study context, resources and support; relocation is optional and unnecessary for most goals.
- Affordable participation: accessible/free alternatives and no required lifestyle purchases.
- Stress/capacity changes should trigger an offered adjustment, never an automatic negative character judgment or forced increase in demands.
- Social connections can support belonging, enjoyment and care as well as ambition. Keep shared accountability optional and selective.
- Digital intentionality: support user-chosen boundaries while avoiding attention-capturing patterns inside Takeover itself.
- Values and trade-offs: ask what matters and what the user wants to protect; disclose which optional activities a proposed plan would displace.
- Borrow principles from role models through small reviewed experiments; personalize the method and assess usefulness rather than copying a lifestyle.
- Relationships: support clear boundaries and user judgment; never make social isolation or relocation a default requirement for progress.
- Honest milestones: acknowledge day-40 completion and offer maintenance, changed focus, a voluntary new cycle, or a break. Avoid automatic escalation or a permanently moving finish line.
- Real-world deadlines: account for school, work, caregiving and temporary high-demand periods; suggested adjustments need the user's choice.
- Optional focused work: purpose, bounded blocks, protected responsibilities, review and an end or exit; no continuous-work or social-isolation requirement.
- Core commitments: explicit and editable actions with realistic frequencies and alternatives; a missed action does not erase progress.
- Financial fit: user-chosen money habits and affordability; no automatic investment allocation, earnings promises or mandatory financial-account access.
- Capacity through support: simplify or share work by agreement where useful; paid help and business administration are optional specialized interests.
- Social support: prioritize mutual respect, care and chosen connection; no wealth-based person ranking, loyalty diagnosis or deterministic friendship formula.
- Source coverage: recaps and tables of contents are inventories. Do not fill missing instruction with invented attribution. Slide 40 is absent.
